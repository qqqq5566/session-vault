The problem

Saved tabs shouldn't vanish.

If you keep dozens of tabs for research, work, or "read later," you may have run into one of these:

⟳

Gone after an update

Browser crashes, disk cleaners or extension updates can wipe locally stored tabs.

⚡

Lost in a crash

Chrome crashes or the computer restarts, and weeks of carefully kept tabs don't come back.

☁

Sync glitches

A sync conflict or browser reset quietly overwrites your sessions with an older or empty copy.

▢

The wrong window

"Restore" brings back a different window than the one you needed, or only part of it.

Already using a tab manager?

OneTab and Session Buddy already have backups. Why this?

Session Vault backs up the tabs you currently have open. Like any extension's data, its snapshots live inside the browser, so they can be lost with your profile, an uninstall or a cleanup tool. That's why the core of Session Vault is your backups as files stored outside the browser, exported in one click. If you already use OneTab's or Session Buddy's backups, keep using them. Session Vault isn't meant to replace your tab manager.

If you use OneTab

OneTab's backup covers tabs you've saved into OneTab, not the windows you currently have open. Its optional Backup & Sync service keeps an end-to-end encrypted cloud backup of those saved tabs. Session Vault would focus on the tabs you still have open, with backups you can keep as files.

If you use Session Buddy

Since version 4.1, Session Buddy automatically keeps local backups of your collections and history inside the browser, and you can also save a backup file by hand. Session Vault is being built around files: one-click exports of your open tabs that you keep outside the browser.

What I'm planning for Session Vault

  • Your backups as files outside the browser: one-click export to JSON or HTML, kept wherever you like.
  • Automatic snapshots of your open windows, with no button to remember.
  • Version history: restore an earlier snapshot, not just the latest one.
  • Local-first: snapshots stay on your device by default.
  • Works alongside your existing tool. Nothing to switch or migrate.

A browser extension can't read another extension's storage, so Session Vault won't touch your OneTab or Session Buddy data. It backs up your open tabs on its own, and I plan to let it import files you've exported from other tab managers. Details about other products are based on their official websites as of October 2026.
